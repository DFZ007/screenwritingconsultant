What these logline examples have in common

Almost every one fits in a single sentence of about 15 to 30 words, and most never name the main character. They describe who the protagonist is, what they want, and what stands in the way, and that’s enough for you to see the whole movie.

  • A character defined by one trait. A lawyer who lies for a living. A cynical weatherman. A man who can’t form new memories.
  • A clear goal or problem. Get home for Thanksgiving. Find the groom. Clean up the city.
  • Built-in conflict or irony. The liar who can’t lie, the thief hired to plant an idea instead of stealing one, the shark at the summer resort.
  • A hint of the genre and tone. You can tell Home Alone is a comedy and The Shining isn’t without being told.

The weaker ones run long, list too many characters, or describe the setup without the conflict. For the full method, read how to write a logline, and if you’re still looking for an idea, start with the 18 types of movie ideas.

Frequently asked questions

What is a logline?

A logline is a one-sentence summary of a movie or series that tells a reader who the protagonist is, what they want and what stands in their way.

How long should a logline be?

One sentence, usually around 25 to 30 words. Most of the logline examples on this page are shorter than that.

What makes a good logline?

A protagonist defined by a clear trait, a specific goal, a strong obstacle and a hint of irony or tone. If a reader can picture the movie from one sentence, it works.
