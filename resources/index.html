Guilds & industry organizations

Start here. The guilds publish more free, reliable information than most paid courses: pay minimums, agency lists, credit rules and craft events.

Writers Guild of America

  • WGA West: FAQ

    Plain answers on joining the guild, finding agents, registration and credits.

    Free

  • WGA Schedule of Minimums

    The minimum a guild signatory must pay for every kind of film and TV writing job, updated for the 2026 contract.

    Free

    Matt’s note Know these numbers before your first deal conversation. You can’t negotiate well if you don’t know the floor.

  • WGA Credits Manuals

    How screen and television writing credit is actually determined, including arbitration.

    Free

  • WGA West Agency List

    Searchable list of guild-franchised agencies that represent writers.

    Free

    Matt’s note A legitimate agent never charges a reading fee. If someone wants money up front to represent you, walk away.

  • WGA East Agency List

    Agencies that signed the guild’s Code of Conduct. Legitimate agents never charge fees beyond commission.

    Free

  • WGA Pilot Deal & Series Comp Guide

    Real-world data on what TV writers are being paid for pilots and series work.

    Free

  • WGA 101 Greatest Screenplays & TV Series

    The guild members’ own lists of the best-written films and shows: a ready-made reading list.

    Free

  • WGA East Events

    Craft talks and workshops from the East Coast guild, some open to the public.

    Partly free

  • WGA West on YouTube

    Panels and craft conversations with working guild members.

    Free

Writers Guild Foundation

  • WGF Resource Center

    A vetted hub of craft videos, TV and feature primers, fellowship listings and advice on bios and personal statements.

  • WGF Events

    Public panels and library office hours with major writers. You don’t need to be a guild member.

    Partly free

  • WGF on YouTube

    Recorded panels and oral histories with film and TV writers.

    Free

  • Veterans Writing Project

    A free year-long writing mentorship for U.S. military veterans.

    Free

Read produced scripts

Reading professional scripts is the single best free education there is. Official sources come first; community libraries are useful but aren’t licensed, and drafts vary.

Official and authorized

  • BBC Writers Script Library

    Hundreds of produced BBC TV, film and radio scripts, posted for writers to study, including Doctor Who.

    FreeUK

  • John August Library

    Free scripts from the writer of Go and Big Fish, plus Chernobyl and Station Eleven posted with their creators’ permission.

    Free

  • Deadline: Read the Screenplay

    Every awards season, Deadline posts studio-supplied screenplays for the year’s contenders.

    FreeSeasonal

  • Netflix FYC: Film

    Netflix’s awards site, with scripts for its film contenders during awards season.

    FreeSeasonal

  • Netflix FYC: Series

    Episode scripts for Netflix’s Emmy contenders.

    FreeSeasonalTV

  • Amazon MGM Studios Guilds

    Official screenplay PDFs for Amazon MGM’s awards films.

    FreeSeasonal

  • Universal Pictures Awards

    Has hosted screenplays for films like Oppenheimer and Wicked; returns each fall.

    FreeSeasonal

  • Disney Studios Awards

    Disney’s awards portal, which has included screenplays in past seasons.

    FreeSeasonal

Research libraries (read on site)

  • WGF Shavelson-Webb Library

    More than 50,000 produced film and TV scripts, plus treatments, pitches and bibles. Los Angeles, by appointment.

    FreeIn person

  • Margaret Herrick Library

    The Academy’s library holds scripts for 15,000+ films. Beverly Hills reading room.

    In person

Community libraries

  • Script Slug

    The cleanest, most current library of film and TV script PDFs. Posted for education; honors takedown requests.

    FreeFan-hosted

    Matt’s note Read the script, then watch the film. The changes between page and screen are where you learn the most.

  • SimplyScripts

    Long-running, actively updated index that links out to scripts, including studio FYC copies, and labels which draft each is.

    FreeFan-hosted

  • IMSDb

    The Internet Movie Script Database. Huge, but drafts and sources vary, so check against the produced film.

    FreeFan-hosted

  • The Daily Script

    Older archive of film and TV scripts; little added in recent years.

    FreeFan-hosted

TV bibles & pitch documents

    FreeTV

  • 8FLiX: series bibles and pitches (part 1)

    FreeFan-hostedTV

  • 8FLiX: series bibles and pitches (part 2)

    FreeFan-hostedTV

  • Battlestar Galactica series bible

    Ronald D. Moore’s 2003 bible for the reimagined series, a model of how to pitch a world.

    FreeFan-hostedTV

  • Star Trek: The Next Generation writers’ guide

    Gene Roddenberry’s 1987 guide for writers on the series.

    FreeFan-hostedTV

  • The Wire: David Simon’s bible

    Kottke’s post with David Simon’s 2000 pitch document for The Wire, alongside several scripts.

    FreeFan-hostedTV

  • WGF Library (bibles on site)

    The Writers Guild Foundation collects show bibles and pitches you can read in person in Los Angeles.

    FreeIn personTV

Fellowships & labs

The programs with real track records of launching careers. Deadlines are typical windows, so confirm the current cycle on each official site.

Feature film

  • Academy Nicholl Fellowships

    Up to five $35,000 fellowships for original features. Since 2025, entries go through partner portals (the Black List and the Writers Guild Foundation). Opens June.

    Entry feeFilm

  • Sundance Screenwriters Lab

    The most prestigious development lab for first- and second-time feature writers. One application covers the Lab, the online Intensive and the Sloan grant. Spring.

    Entry feeFilm

  • Film Independent Screenwriting Lab

    Development lab for emerging feature writers in Los Angeles. Summer.

    Entry feeFilm

  • SFFILM Sloan Science in Cinema Fellowship

    $35,000 for science-themed features or series. February to May.

    Entry feeFilmTV

  • CineStory Feature Retreat & Fellowship

    $10,000 plus a year of mentorship and a writers’ retreat. February to July.

    Entry feeFilm

Television

  • Disney Entertainment TV Writing Program

    A paid staff-writer job for emerging writers with two original pilots. Free to apply. May to June.

    FreeTV

    Matt’s note Free to apply, and it’s a paid staff job at the end. Have two strong original pilots ready, not one.

  • Paramount Writers Mentoring Program

    Formerly the CBS program, now also covering Nickelodeon. Eight months of mentorship. April.

    FreeTV

  • NBC TV Writers Program

    NBCUniversal’s program for emerging episodic writers, formerly Writers on the Verge.

    FreeTV

  • Sundance Episodic Lab

    Lab for original pilots, open internationally. July to August.

    Entry feeTV

  • Women in Film x Black List Episodic Lab

    For women and underrepresented genders in LA. October to November.

    Entry feeTV

  • CAPE New Writers Fellowship

    For Asian and Pacific Islander TV writers. Applications typically in the fall.

    TV

  • CineStory TV Retreat & Fellowship

    For pilots: a cash prize, mentorship and retreat. August to December.

    Entry feeTV

Film and TV

  • Sundance Native Lab

    For Indigenous writers of features or series. Free to apply. December.

    FreeFilmTV

  • Film Independent Project Involve

    Year-long program for filmmakers and writers from underrepresented communities. May to July.

    Entry feeFilmTV

  • Stowe Story Labs

    Labs and retreats for emerging feature and TV writers, with rolling deadlines from October.

    Entry feeFilmTV

  • The Writers Lab (NYWIFT)

    For women and nonbinary writers over 40, features or pilots. February to April.

    FilmTV

International

    The BBC reads unsolicited drama scripts from new writers. Free. Usually November to December.

    FreeUK

    A paid five-month course for UK writers with no TV or feature credits. Free to apply. Autumn.

    FreeUK

  • Berlinale Talents

    The Berlin festival’s program for emerging film and TV creatives. July to September.

  • TorinoFilmLab

Screenplay contests worth entering

Most contests are a business, not a door. These are the few with real track records of getting writers read, staffed or sold.

Most respected

  • Academy Nicholl Fellowships

    The gold standard, run by the Academy. Entries through the Black List or WGF portals, June to July, about $100.

    Entry feeFilm

  • Austin Film Festival Script Competition

    Long the top “writers’ festival,” with feature, teleplay and short categories and a great conference. March to May, $50 to $90.

    Entry feeFilmTV

  • Script Pipeline

    Strong record of placing winners with producers and studios, with development support after the contest.

    Entry feeFilmTV

  • PAGE International Screenwriting Awards

    Running since 2003, with alumni including the creators of Ozark and Pose. Check the site for the current cycle.

    Entry feeFilmTV

Solid

  • Final Draft Big Break

    Feature, TV and short categories with $10,000 grand prizes and industry meetings. March to June.

    Entry feeFilmTV

  • Slamdance Screenplay Competition

    From the indie festival; every entrant gets short reader notes. March to August.

    Entry feeFilmTV

  • BlueCat Screenplay Competition

    Valued mainly because every entry gets written feedback. June to November.

    Entry feeFilm

Protect your work & know the business

Register before you send your script out, and know what fair pay looks like.

  • U.S. Copyright Office registration

    The strongest legal protection for a script. Online registration is $45 for a single author and work.

    Paid

    Matt’s note Register with the Copyright Office, not just a guild registry. The registry proves a date; copyright registration is what gives you real legal standing.

  • WGA East Registry

    Dated proof of authorship for 10 years. $25 for the public, $10 for members.

    Paid

  • WGA West Registry

    Dated proof of authorship for 5 years, open to non-members.

    Paid

  • WGA Minimum Basic Agreement

    The guild’s master contract with the studios, with plain-language summaries.

    Free

A note on these links

This is an independent list; none of these organizations is affiliated with or endorses this site. Programs, fees and deadlines change every year, so always confirm on the official site before you apply. Coverfly, ScreenCraft, WeScreenplay, The Script Lab and the Tracking Board have all shut down; be wary of old articles that still recommend them. Community script libraries and fan-hosted bibles are linked for study only and are not officially licensed.
